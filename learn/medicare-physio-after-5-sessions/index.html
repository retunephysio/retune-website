Used your 5 Medicare physio sessions? What happens next

Written and reviewed by a registered physiotherapistReviewed 30 October 2026Updated 30 October 2026

In short

  • With a GP chronic condition management plan, Medicare gives a rebate for up to 5 allied health visits a calendar year. That 5 is shared across physio, podiatry, dietitians and other allied health.
  • After the 5 visits you can keep seeing your physio, but Medicare won't give a rebate for more visits under the plan until the count resets on 1 January.
  • Many people use the funded visits to set up a home program, then keep it going themselves. Planning for that from your first visit makes it easier.

If you live with a long-term condition, your GP may have written you a care plan so Medicare helps pay for physio. Five visits can go quickly, especially if you also see a podiatrist or dietitian. This page explains how the Medicare rules work in 2026 and how people keep their exercises going once the funded visits are used.

Medicare rules change. This page was checked against MBS Online on the date at the top. Your GP, your physio or Services Australia can confirm what applies to you.

How the Medicare plan works

From 1 July 2025, the plan your GP writes is called a GP chronic condition management plan. You may still hear the older names: care plan, CDM plan, GP Management Plan (GPMP) or Team Care Arrangement (TCA).2

  • Who it's for. People with a chronic (long-term) condition whose GP thinks a plan is right for them.1
  • How many visits. Up to 5 allied health visits each calendar year (January to December). The 5 are shared, so a physio visit and a podiatry visit both count towards the same 5.1
  • What Medicare pays. From 1 July 2026, the Medicare benefit for a physio visit under the plan is $63.40. Each visit must be at least 20 minutes. Clinics set their own fees, so you may pay a gap.1
  • Referral. Your GP gives you a written referral. For referrals written from 1 July 2025, it lasts for the time stated on it, or 18 months from your first visit if no time is stated.1
  • Where to get the plan. If you're registered with MyMedicare, the plan is done at the practice you're registered with. If not, your usual GP does it.2
  • Older plans. A GPMP and TCA made before 1 July 2025 can still be used until 30 June 2027.1,2

Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people may be able to get extra allied health visits under separate items. Ask your GP.1

What happens after the 5 visits

You can keep seeing your physio. Medicare just won't give a rebate for more allied health visits under the plan that year. The count goes back to zero on 1 January, and you don't need a new plan just because the year has changed.1

It's worth knowing that 5 is a funding limit, not a clinical judgement about how many visits you need. Some people need fewer, some need more.

How the 5 visits might be spread out

There's no single right way to use the visits. Your physio will usually suggest a pattern based on your condition, your goals and how confident you feel doing things on your own. Two common approaches:

  • Closer together first, then further apart. For example, weekly visits early on to get the exercises right and adjust them as you go, then the last one or two spread over the following months. This keeps someone checking in on your progress for months, not just the first five weeks.
  • Spread out from the start. For example, a visit every few weeks or every month or two. This can suit people who are already fairly confident with exercise, or whose condition changes slowly.

If you're not sure which suits you, it's a good question to ask at your first visit. Knowing the plan for all 5 helps you see what you're working towards between each one.

Making the 5 visits count

Many people, and many physios, use the funded visits to set up a plan the person can carry on with at home. Some ideas to raise with your physio:

  1. Ask for a home plan you can follow. Written instructions, photos or videos of your exercises, and how often to do them.
  2. Agree on a short version. Ask what a shorter session should look like on a busy or sore day, so you're not guessing.
  3. Know when to check back. Ask which changes in pain or symptoms mean you should book in sooner than planned.
  4. Think about a later review. Some people keep a visit for a check-in later in the year.

Keeping it going on your own

Doing exercises without regular appointments is hard for most people. Reviews of home exercise programmes find that many people don't keep them up, and that confidence, past exercise habits and support all play a part.4 For people with hip or knee osteoarthritis, a review found that follow-up "booster" sessions with a physiotherapist helped people keep up their exercises.3 That's one reason a spaced-out review visit can be worth planning for.

Things that tend to help:

  • tie your exercises to something you already do every day (see the "After I…" plan);
  • keep a simple record of what you've done, so you can see it and share it at your next visit;
  • have a plan for missed days (see getting going again).

Other ways to pay for more physio

  • Private health extras. If you have extras cover, it may pay towards visits outside the Medicare plan. You can't claim Medicare and your extras for the same visit. See when your extras run out.
  • Other schemes. If your condition relates to a work injury, a car accident, Defence service or a disability plan, a different scheme (workers compensation, CTP insurance, DVA or the NDIS) may cover treatment under its own rules.
  • Paying yourself. Some clinics offer shorter review appointments or group exercise classes. Ask what options your clinic has.
