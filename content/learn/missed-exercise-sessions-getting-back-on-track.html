Everyone who builds a routine misses sessions. A bad night's sleep, a sick child, a busy week at work, a sore day. The missed day itself is rarely the problem. The problem is what often comes next: "I've already missed three days, so what's the point?"

A missed day matters less than you think

In a well-known study of how habits form, people built a new daily habit over 12 weeks and rated how automatic it felt. Missing one opportunity didn't make much difference to how the habit developed over time.1

That fits with what physios see too. Progress usually comes from what you do across weeks and months, not from any single day. A few gaps in an otherwise steady routine are normal.

Why restarting feels harder than it should

  • All-or-nothing thinking. If the plan was "every day", one miss can feel like the plan has failed. It hasn't. It just needs to start again.
  • The bar feels higher. After a break, a full session can feel like a big ask, so it gets put off again.
  • The cue got lost. If you were away or your routine changed, the trigger you used may not be there any more.

How to get going again

  1. Restart at your usual cue. Go back to the moment you used before: "After I pour my morning coffee…". Repeating the same cue is what builds the habit.4
  2. Make the first session small. A short version, such as one set of each exercise, is enough to restart. Simple, consistent routines are linked with habits forming.2
  3. Don't try to catch up. Doing three days' worth in one go isn't the same as three separate sessions, and it can leave you sore. Just pick up the normal plan.
  4. Look at what got in the way. Was it time, tiredness, pain, or the cue disappearing? Each one has a different fix.
  5. Count what you have done. Ten sessions in the last month is ten sessions, whatever happened in between. Look at the total, not the gaps.

If pain is the reason

Pain during exercise is one of the common reasons people stop doing their physio exercises.3 If your exercises have been more painful than usual, or pain is getting worse, check with your physio before you restart. They can tell you whether to change anything. Don't guess, and don't push through pain that's getting worse.

Plan for next time

A simple "if… then…" line can make the next restart easier:

If I miss a day, I'll do the short version at my usual time the next day.

Writing it down now, while it's easy to think clearly, takes the decision out of the moment when it's hard.
