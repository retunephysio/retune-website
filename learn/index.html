Keeping your exercise going

Plain-English guides on building an exercise habit and keeping up your rehab once appointments finish. Each one explains what published research has found, with the sources listed.

How these are written: each guide is written and reviewed by a registered physiotherapist, uses published research (listed at the end of each page), and is checked again when the "Updated" date changes. They're general information, not advice about your situation.

  • How to keep your exercises going after physio ends

    Why home exercises often slip once physio appointments finish, and what research suggests helps: small routines, everyday cues and a plan for busy days.

    Updated 29 October 2026

  • Why it's hard to stick with exercise (it isn't willpower)

    Motivation comes and goes, so routines built on it often fade. Here's what research says about habits, identity and making exercise easier to keep.

    Updated 29 October 2026

  • The "After I…" plan: tie your exercises to something you already do

    Linking exercise to an everyday cue, such as "after I pour my coffee", is one of the best-studied ways to build a habit. How it works and how to set one up.

    Updated 29 October 2026

  • How long does it take to build an exercise habit?

    Not 21 days. Studies suggest about two months on average, with big differences between people. What the research found and what it means for you.

    Updated 29 October 2026

  • Missed a few days of exercises? How to get going again

    Missing a day or a week is normal. Research suggests one missed day barely affects a forming habit. Practical, blame-free ways to restart your routine.

    Updated 29 October 2026
