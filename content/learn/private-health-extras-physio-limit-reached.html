You're partway through rehab, and the clinic tells you your extras limit for physio has run out. It's a common point for people to stop, even if they meant to keep going. This page explains how extras limits usually work, and how people keep their exercises going until the limit resets.

Every policy is different. The details below are general. Your fund's app, your policy documents or the fund itself can tell you exactly what applies to you.

How extras limits usually work

  • A benefit per visit. Most policies pay a set amount per physio visit, often more for the first visit than for later ones.1
  • A yearly limit. Benefits are capped at a yearly maximum. On some policies physio has its own limit; on others it shares a combined limit with services such as chiro or osteo.1
  • A reset date. Most funds reset extras limits on 1 January. Some reset on 1 July, and a few on the anniversary of when you joined.1,2
  • Little or no rollover. Unused limits rarely carry over to the next year, though a few funds allow some rollover, and some policies increase limits the longer you've been a member.1,2

Every policy has a Private Health Information Statement that sets out what it covers. You can look up any policy on the government's privatehealth.gov.au site.3

Check before you stop

  • When does your limit reset? If it's only a few weeks away, you may be able to plan your next visit for just after.
  • Is it a physio limit or a combined limit? If it's shared, other services may have used some of it.
  • Do you have a Medicare plan? If you have a long-term condition, your GP may be able to write a plan that gives a Medicare rebate for some physio visits. You generally can't claim Medicare and your extras for the same visit, but they can cover different visits. See Medicare and physio.
  • Does another scheme apply? Work injuries, car accidents, Defence service and NDIS plans each have their own rules for paying for treatment.

Keeping your rehab going in between

In most rehab, a lot of the work happens at home between visits anyway. A review of ways to help people stick with physio exercises looked at things like written instructions, goal setting and follow-up, and found some promising but mixed results.4 These ideas are worth trying:

  1. Ask for a home plan before the limit runs out. If you know your last funded visit is coming, tell your physio. Ask for clear written instructions, photos or videos, and how often to do each exercise.
  2. Agree on a short version. Ask what a shorter session should look like for busy or sore days, so you're not guessing what's safe to cut.
  3. Ask which signs mean you should come back. For example, pain that's getting worse, new symptoms, or exercises that feel much too easy or too hard.
  4. Tie it to your day. Repeating something after the same daily cue is how habits form.5 See the "After I…" plan.
  5. Plan one review for after the reset. Booking it now gives you a date to work towards and a chance to check your progress and update your exercises.
  6. Keep a simple record. A calendar, a notebook or an app. Bringing it to your next visit makes the most of the time you have.

When not to wait

A limit running out is a money question. It shouldn't decide whether you get help for a problem. If your pain is getting worse, you have new symptoms, or you're not sure an exercise is still right for you, contact your physio or GP rather than waiting for the reset.
