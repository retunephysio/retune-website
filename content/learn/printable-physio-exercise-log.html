How to use it

  1. Fill in the left columns once. Copy each exercise from your physio's sheet, with the sets and reps, or how long to hold.
  2. Mark each day. A tick for a full session, S for a short version. Both count. Leave the box blank if it didn't happen.
  3. Add a word about how it felt at the bottom of the day, and note what got in the way on days you missed. Only fill in the pain row if your physio has asked you to keep a score.
  4. Look back at the end of the week. Which days worked? What got in the way? Start a fresh page and bring the old ones to your next review.

Why write it down?

A large review of 138 experiments found that people who monitored their progress more often were more likely to reach their goal, and the effect was bigger when progress was physically recorded rather than just thought about.1 For physio exercises, recording what you do is usually one part of a wider approach, alongside clear instructions, a plan that suits your day and follow-up with your physio.2

For more on what's worth tracking, see how to track your physio exercises at home.

Tips that make it stick

  • Keep it where you exercise. On the fridge, by the bed, or next to the mat. If you have to go looking for it, it won't get filled in.
  • Fill it in straight away. Right after you finish, while it's fresh.
  • Plan your short version now. Deciding in advance what a busy-day version looks like makes it much easier to do something rather than nothing.
  • Blank boxes are information. They show you which days need a different plan. They're not a mark against you.
